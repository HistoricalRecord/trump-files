JD Vance

Full name: James David Vance

Classification: Vice President of the United States; author; former United States senator; former venture capital professional.

JD Vance emerged nationally through his 2016 memoir, Hillbilly Elegy, which examined his upbringing in Middletown, Ohio, his family's Appalachian background, his service in the United States Marine Corps, his education at Ohio State University and Yale Law School, and his later work in law and venture capital. Before entering electoral politics, Vance was known primarily as an author and political commentator. His public identity also changed over time: he was born James David Bowman and subsequently adopted the surname Vance, associated with his maternal grandparents. The name used publicly throughout his political career has been J.D. Vance.

Vance's relationship with Donald Trump changed substantially over the course of the 2016-2024 political period. During the 2016 campaign Vance was publicly critical of Trump, describing him in highly negative terms and expressing concern about the political movement surrounding him. In later years Vance became a Trump supporter, sought Trump's endorsement during his 2022 Ohio Senate campaign, and became one of Trump's prominent political allies. Trump endorsed Vance's Senate campaign, and in July 2024 Trump selected Vance as his vice-presidential running mate. Vance subsequently became vice president following the 2024 election. The contrast between Vance's earlier criticism and later alliance is an important part of his political biography.

Vance's record therefore contains two distinct periods that should be preserved separately: his pre-Trump and early-Trump skepticism, and his subsequent transformation into one of Trump's closest political allies. His earlier writings and interviews provide primary material for examining that change, while his Senate and vice-presidential records document his later institutional relationship with the Trump administration.

Jared Kushner

Classification: Senior presidential adviser; businessman; publisher; real-estate executive; Trump family member by marriage.

Jared Kushner is the husband of Ivanka Trump and became one of Donald Trump's closest family and political advisers during Trump's first presidential administration. Before entering government, Kushner worked in the family real-estate business and acquired the New York Observer. His political prominence increased dramatically after Trump's 2016 election, when he received a senior White House advisory role. His portfolio included Middle East policy, criminal-justice initiatives, government modernization and other major policy areas.

Kushner's connection to Trump is therefore both familial and institutional. As Trump's son-in-law, he occupied an unusual position inside the first administration and participated in some of its most consequential diplomatic initiatives, including the administration's Middle East diplomacy and the Abraham Accords. His proximity to the president also generated recurring scrutiny concerning the boundary between family relationships, private business interests and public duties.

After leaving the White House, Kushner returned to the private sector and founded Affinity Partners, an investment firm. His post-government activities, relationships with foreign investors and continued association with the Trump political network have made him a recurring subject for archival research concerning the relationship between political office, family networks and private capital.

Ivanka Trump

Classification: Business executive; author; presidential adviser; daughter of Donald Trump.

Ivanka Trump is Donald Trump's daughter and the wife of Jared Kushner. Before her father's presidency she worked in the Trump Organization and developed a public profile through business, media appearances and a fashion brand. During the 2016 presidential campaign she became one of Trump's most visible family surrogates and participated in his political operation.

During Trump's first presidency, Ivanka served as a senior adviser in the White House. Her portfolio included economic policy, workforce issues, paid-family-leave proposals and women's economic initiatives. Her position placed her alongside Kushner at the center of the family presence inside the administration.

Her role also produced questions about conflicts between governmental responsibilities and the family's private commercial interests. Those questions form an important part of the historical record because Ivanka simultaneously belonged to Trump's immediate family, had an established private business background and held an official White House position.

Donald Trump Jr.

Classification: Business executive; political surrogate; author; son of Donald Trump.

Donald Trump Jr. is the eldest son of Donald Trump and became increasingly prominent in Republican politics during and after the 2016 presidential campaign. He worked in the Trump Organization before becoming a major political surrogate for his father. His public role has included campaign appearances, speeches, fundraising and extensive social-media activity.

Trump Jr. became particularly important to the Trump movement because he frequently communicated directly with the president's political base. His public statements, interviews and social-media posts became part of the broader political communication ecosystem surrounding the Trump presidency.

His name also appears prominently in records concerning the 2016 campaign and the Trump Tower meeting involving Russian attorney Natalia Veselnitskaya. The Senate Intelligence Committee investigated the meeting and related campaign contacts. The episode remains an important component of the documentary record surrounding the 2016 campaign.

Eric Trump

Classification: Business executive; political surrogate; son of Donald Trump.

Eric Trump is Donald Trump's third child and worked extensively in the Trump Organization. Along with Donald Trump Jr., he became involved in managing the family business while his father served as president. Eric has also been a regular campaign surrogate and public defender of his father's political agenda.

During the first Trump administration, Eric Trump remained outside formal government office while continuing to operate within the family's business structure. This distinction is significant for the historical record because the Trump family's commercial organization continued operating while the family's patriarch occupied the presidency.

Eric's public appearances, interviews and campaign activity provide a substantial documentary record of how the Trump family's political message was communicated beyond the formal White House apparatus.

Elon Musk

Classification: Technology executive; political donor; owner of X; DOGE adviser; Trump political ally during the 2024 campaign and early second administration.

Elon Musk's relationship with Donald Trump developed after years in which Musk was not consistently aligned with Trump. Musk publicly criticized Trump at various points, supported Ron DeSantis during the 2024 Republican primary period, and then endorsed Trump in July 2024 following the assassination attempt against Trump. Musk subsequently became one of the largest financial supporters of Trump's presidential campaign effort through America PAC and became a highly visible political surrogate.

America PAC was created in 2024 as a pro-Trump super PAC backed by Musk. FactCheck.org documented substantial Musk financing of the organization and its involvement in voter-contact and turnout operations. Contemporary reporting placed Musk's political spending at an unusually large scale. The historical record should distinguish documented spending and political activity from broader claims that any individual donor "bought" an election; the former can be documented, while the latter is an evaluative characterization rather than an established fact.

Following Trump's return to office, Musk became closely associated with the Department of Government Efficiency, commonly called DOGE. The White House created the DOGE structure by executive order and described its purpose as modernization, efficiency and productivity in the federal government. Musk participated in the administration's efforts to reduce federal spending and workforce levels before leaving his formal DOGE role. The White House documented Trump and Musk together in the Oval Office on May 30, 2025.

Musk's importance to this archive extends beyond his formal governmental position. He controlled a major social-media platform, contributed heavily to Trump's political operation, participated in administration policy discussions and became one of the most prominent private-sector figures associated with the second Trump administration.

Steve Bannon

Classification: Political strategist; former White House chief strategist; media executive.

Steve Bannon became one of the most consequential ideological figures associated with Trump's first presidential campaign. Before joining Trump's campaign, Bannon operated Breitbart News and had become associated with nationalist and populist political currents. He joined Trump's campaign in 2016 and later became White House chief strategist.

Bannon's tenure inside the White House was short compared with the duration of Trump's first presidency, but his influence continued after his departure. He remained a vocal defender of Trump and later worked through media appearances, political organizing and activist networks to support Trump's movement.

Bannon's legal history is also relevant. Trump issued a presidential pardon for Bannon in January 2021 in connection with a federal fraud case involving the We Build the Wall fundraising operation. Bannon later faced a separate contempt of Congress case related to the January 6 investigation, demonstrating why his record should distinguish separate legal matters rather than treating them as one proceeding.

Mike Pence

Classification: Former vice president; governor; former member of Congress.

Mike Pence served as Donald Trump's vice-presidential running mate in 2016 and as vice president from 2017 through January 2021. Before joining Trump's ticket, Pence had spent years in Republican politics as a member of the U.S. House of Representatives and governor of Indiana. His selection gave the Trump campaign a candidate with extensive ties to traditional conservative Republican institutions.

Pence's relationship with Trump became increasingly complicated during the final months of the first administration. The most significant break concerned the January 6, 2021 electoral certification. Pence maintained that the Constitution did not give the vice president unilateral authority to reject certified electoral votes. His refusal to do so marked a significant institutional disagreement with Trump.

The relationship subsequently became openly adversarial in portions of the Republican primary and post-presidential period. Pence nevertheless remained a major historical figure in the Trump administration because he served as Trump's constitutional successor, presided over the Senate and was present during the transition from the Trump presidency to the Biden presidency.

Jeff Sessions

Classification: Former United States senator; first attorney general of the Trump administration.

Jeff Sessions was an early and important political supporter of Donald Trump's 2016 campaign. A United States senator from Alabama and a prominent conservative figure, Sessions became one of Trump's earliest high-profile congressional allies. His endorsement helped establish Trump's relationships with portions of the Republican establishment during the campaign.

Trump nominated Sessions to become attorney general after the 2016 election. Sessions subsequently recused himself from the federal investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election because of his own campaign contacts. The recusal became a major source of conflict between Sessions and Trump.

Trump repeatedly criticized Sessions publicly and eventually accepted his resignation in November 2018. The episode is one of the clearest examples of a senior Trump ally becoming the subject of sustained presidential criticism after entering government. Sessions later attempted a political comeback in Alabama but lost the 2020 Republican Senate primary to Tommy Tuberville, who had Trump's endorsement.

William P. Barr

Classification: Former attorney general of the United States; former deputy attorney general.

William Barr served as attorney general during the later part of Trump's first administration. He had previously served as attorney general under President George H. W. Bush and entered the Trump administration with a long-established reputation in federal law enforcement and constitutional law.

Barr became closely associated with several of the most consequential legal disputes of the Trump presidency, including the Mueller investigation and the administration's response to the report. Barr publicly defended the president on numerous occasions and became a prominent figure in Trump's legal and political orbit.

Their relationship eventually fractured. In December 2020, Barr said the Justice Department had not found fraud on a scale that could have changed the outcome of the 2020 presidential election. He left office shortly afterward. His departure illustrates the distinction between political alignment and complete agreement with a president's claims.

James Mattis

Classification: Former secretary of defense; retired United States Marine Corps general.

James Mattis entered the Trump administration with an unusually strong military reputation. A retired Marine general with extensive command experience, Mattis became Trump's secretary of defense in 2017. His appointment was viewed as a bridge between Trump's unconventional political movement and the established national-security establishment.

Mattis eventually became one of the most prominent former Trump officials to publicly criticize the president. His resignation followed Trump's decision concerning U.S. forces in Syria and disagreements about national-security policy. Mattis remained outside electoral politics but later issued unusually direct criticism of Trump's leadership and response to domestic unrest.

Mattis is therefore important to the personnel archive as an example of a senior official whose professional relationship with Trump deteriorated substantially while he was still serving in the administration.

John F. Kelly

Classification: Former White House chief of staff; retired United States Marine Corps general.

John Kelly served as secretary of homeland security before becoming Trump's White House chief of staff in 2017. His selection represented another attempt to impose military-style structure and discipline on the White House after a turbulent opening period of the administration.

Kelly's tenure involved numerous internal disputes and controversies surrounding White House operations. He eventually left the administration in 2018. After leaving government, Kelly became one of several former senior Trump officials whose accounts of the administration differed substantially from the president's public presentation of events.

His later public comments and reported recollections have made him an important source for researchers examining internal White House decision-making and Trump's relationships with senior military and national-security officials.

John Bolton

Classification: Former national security adviser; diplomat; author.

John Bolton served as Trump's national security adviser from 2018 to 2019. Before entering the administration, Bolton had served in senior diplomatic and national-security positions in Republican administrations and had become known for interventionist foreign-policy positions.

Bolton's relationship with Trump deteriorated over major foreign-policy disagreements. Trump removed him from the national security adviser position in September 2019. Bolton later published a memoir describing his time in the White House, which became the subject of a major legal and political dispute.

The Bolton record is particularly useful for documenting the difference between a senior official's public role while inside an administration and his subsequent account of events after leaving government.

Rex Tillerson

Classification: Former secretary of state; former ExxonMobil chief executive.

Rex Tillerson was selected by Trump to serve as secretary of state after a career leading ExxonMobil. His appointment reflected Trump's interest in recruiting executives and outsiders with extensive international business experience. Tillerson became the administration's principal diplomat during its first major period of foreign-policy activity.

The relationship between Tillerson and Trump became increasingly strained. Differences over foreign policy, staffing and the operation of the State Department contributed to tensions between the two men. Trump dismissed Tillerson as secretary of state in March 2018.

Tillerson's tenure is particularly important for understanding the turnover of Trump's first administration and the repeated difficulty of maintaining stable relationships between Trump and senior cabinet officials.

Nikki Haley

Classification: Former United Nations ambassador; former South Carolina governor; 2024 Republican presidential candidate.

Nikki Haley became one of Trump's highest-profile cabinet-level appointments when he selected her as ambassador to the United Nations. Before joining his administration she had served two terms as governor of South Carolina and had developed a national profile within the Republican Party.

Haley's relationship with Trump changed after she left the administration. She subsequently launched a presidential campaign for the 2024 Republican nomination, placing herself directly in competition with Trump. During the primary campaign the two repeatedly criticized one another. After Trump secured the Republican nomination, Haley did not immediately endorse him, though she later said she would vote for him.

Her record provides a useful example of a former Trump cabinet official becoming a presidential rival and illustrates the distinction between serving within a presidential administration and competing against its former president for the party's nomination.

Ron DeSantis

Classification: Governor of Florida; former U.S. representative; 2024 Republican presidential candidate.

Ron DeSantis developed a close political relationship with Trump during his rise in Florida politics. Trump endorsed DeSantis for governor in 2018, and DeSantis became one of the most prominent Republican governors associated with the Trump political movement.

The relationship changed when DeSantis entered the 2024 Republican presidential primary. He competed directly against Trump and became one of the principal alternatives to Trump in the primary. The campaign produced extensive criticism between the two political organizations and marked a major break from their earlier alliance.

DeSantis suspended his presidential campaign in January 2024 and endorsed Trump. The episode is significant because it documents how political alliances within the Republican Party could shift rapidly between electoral cycles.

Marco Rubio

Classification: U.S. secretary of state; former United States senator; former 2016 presidential candidate.

Marco Rubio and Donald Trump were bitter Republican primary opponents during the 2016 presidential campaign. Rubio was a prominent senator from Florida and represented a more conventional Republican establishment position on several issues. The primary campaign produced repeated personal and political attacks between Rubio and Trump.

After Trump won the presidency, the relationship changed. Rubio remained in the Senate and increasingly worked alongside the Trump administration on foreign policy and Republican priorities. Trump subsequently selected Rubio as secretary of state for his second administration, making the former primary rival the nation's senior diplomat.

Rubio's career therefore provides a clear example of the transition from electoral opposition to executive-branch cooperation. His Senate record, 2016 campaign material and State Department record together document that evolution.

Ted Cruz

Classification: U.S. senator; former presidential candidate; conservative legal and political figure.

Ted Cruz was one of Donald Trump's principal opponents during the 2016 Republican presidential primary. The campaign between them became intensely personal, with both candidates attacking one another publicly. Cruz declined to endorse Trump immediately after losing the nomination and initially maintained distance from Trump's campaign.

Cruz eventually endorsed Trump and became an important Senate ally of the Trump administration. He worked closely with the administration on judicial nominations, immigration, deregulation and other Republican priorities.

The change from bitter primary opponent to congressional ally is a significant component of Cruz's political relationship with Trump and illustrates the post-primary consolidation of the Republican Party around Trump's presidency.

Roger Stone

Classification: Political operative; longtime Trump associate; convicted federal defendant; recipient of presidential clemency.

Roger Stone has known Donald Trump since the late twentieth century and became one of the longest-running political associates in Trump's orbit. Stone worked as a Republican political operative and became closely associated with aggressive campaign tactics, opposition research and political media operations.

Stone served as a Trump campaign adviser during the 2016 election before leaving the formal campaign. He later became a central figure in investigations surrounding Russian interference in the 2016 election. A federal jury convicted Stone in 2019 on charges including obstruction, false statements and witness tampering.

Trump commuted Stone's sentence in July 2020 and subsequently granted him a full pardon in December 2020. The sequence makes Stone one of the clearest examples of a longtime personal political associate receiving presidential clemency.

Paul Manafort

Classification: Political consultant; former Trump campaign chairman; federal criminal defendant; recipient of presidential pardon.

Paul Manafort became chairman of Donald Trump's 2016 presidential campaign during a critical period of the Republican primary and general-election campaign. Manafort had decades of experience as a Republican political consultant and had also worked internationally as a political adviser, including extensive work in Ukraine.

Manafort resigned from Trump's campaign in August 2016 after reporting raised questions about his foreign consulting activities and payments. He later became a central defendant in federal investigations conducted by Special Counsel Robert Mueller.

Manafort was convicted in federal court on financial crimes charges and pleaded guilty in another federal case. Trump granted Manafort a full pardon in December 2020. His record is therefore important both to the history of Trump's 2016 campaign and to the subsequent federal investigations of that campaign's personnel.

Michael Flynn

Classification: Retired Army lieutenant general; former national security adviser; recipient of presidential pardon.

Michael Flynn became one of Trump's most visible national security supporters during the 2016 campaign. A retired lieutenant general and former director of the Defense Intelligence Agency, Flynn had developed increasingly controversial political views after leaving government and became a vocal supporter of Trump's campaign.

Trump selected Flynn as national security adviser after the election. Flynn resigned after only a short period in office amid questions concerning his contacts with the Russian ambassador and statements made to the vice president and FBI. He subsequently became a major defendant in the federal investigation of Russian election interference.

Flynn pleaded guilty to making false statements to the FBI, later withdrew that plea, and became the subject of extensive litigation. Trump issued Flynn a full pardon in November 2020. Flynn subsequently remained active in pro-Trump political circles and became a prominent speaker at events associated with Trump's movement.

Ross Ulbricht

Classification: Silk Road creator; federal prisoner; recipient of a full presidential pardon.

Ross William Ulbricht created and operated the Silk Road, an online marketplace that operated through the Tor anonymity network. Federal prosecutors said the site facilitated the sale of illegal drugs and other unlawful goods and services. Ulbricht operated under the pseudonym "Dread Pirate Roberts." He was arrested in San Francisco in October 2013.

In 2015 a federal jury convicted Ulbricht on seven charges associated with the operation of Silk Road, including drug trafficking, continuing criminal enterprise, computer hacking conspiracy, identity-document offenses and money laundering conspiracy. He received two life sentences plus additional concurrent prison terms. The FBI subsequently identified his laptop as a major piece of evidence in the investigation.

Ulbricht became a cause célèbre among advocates of criminal justice reform, cryptocurrency enthusiasts and libertarians. Donald Trump publicly promised during the 2024 campaign to commute Ulbricht's sentence. On January 21, 2025, Trump issued Ulbricht a full and unconditional pardon. The official DOJ warrant records the pardon and the original convictions and sentence.

The Ulbricht record should distinguish the criminal conduct established in court from later political arguments concerning sentencing. It should also distinguish federal allegations concerning murder-for-hire solicitations from actual killings: prosecutors alleged such solicitations, but no murder was carried out as a result of them.

Jeffrey Epstein

Classification: Financier; convicted sex offender; longtime member of elite social networks.

Jeffrey Epstein was a wealthy financier who developed an extensive network of relationships with politicians, executives, academics, celebrities and members of international high society. Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida in 2008 to state charges involving prostitution and a minor and was required to register as a sex offender. In 2019 federal prosecutors in New York charged him with sex trafficking of minors and conspiracy to commit sex trafficking.

Epstein also had a documented social relationship with Donald Trump during the 1980s and 1990s. Trump and Epstein appeared together socially and were photographed at events. In a 2002 profile, Trump described Epstein favorably and commented on Epstein's interest in women "on the younger side." Trump later said that the two had a falling out and that Epstein was no longer welcome at Mar-a-Lago. Trump has denied involvement in Epstein's criminal conduct.

The existence of a social relationship is documented, but it should not be converted into an unsupported claim that Trump participated in Epstein's crimes. The historical record should separately preserve photographs, statements, social connections, court records, victim testimony and investigative reporting so that documented association is not confused with proof of criminal participation.

Ghislaine Maxwell

Classification: Epstein associate; convicted federal sex-trafficking offender.

Ghislaine Maxwell was a longtime associate and former girlfriend of Jeffrey Epstein. She became closely involved in Epstein's social and personal world and was accused by prosecutors of helping recruit and groom underage girls for Epstein. Maxwell was arrested by federal authorities in July 2020 and subsequently prosecuted in the Southern District of New York.

In December 2021, a federal jury convicted Maxwell on multiple counts connected with the sexual exploitation of minors. She was sentenced in 2022 to 20 years in federal prison. Her conviction made her one of the central figures in the federal criminal record surrounding Epstein's trafficking operation.

Maxwell's connection to Trump is primarily through the broader social network surrounding Epstein. Maxwell and Trump moved in overlapping elite social circles during the period when Trump and Epstein were known socially. That association should not be presented as evidence that Trump participated in Maxwell's crimes. The appropriate archival approach is to document the overlapping social network, photographs, public statements, court records and later investigations separately.

Linda McMahon

Classification: Business executive; former WWE executive; former administrator; Trump cabinet official.

Linda McMahon built a national profile through World Wrestling Entertainment alongside her husband Vince McMahon. She later entered Republican politics and ran twice for the United States Senate in Connecticut. McMahon became an important donor and supporter within Trump's political network.

Trump appointed McMahon to lead the Small Business Administration during his first administration. She remained politically close to Trump after leaving the cabinet and later became involved in the Trump transition and second administration.

McMahon's career provides a particularly clear example of the intersection between entertainment, private business, political fundraising and executive-branch appointments within Trump's political network.

Howard Lutnick

Classification: Business executive; Cantor Fitzgerald chairman; commerce secretary; Trump political supporter.

Howard Lutnick is a longtime Wall Street executive and chairman and chief executive of Cantor Fitzgerald. He became increasingly involved in Trump's political operation during the 2024 presidential campaign and served as a prominent fundraiser and supporter.

Trump selected Lutnick to serve as secretary of commerce in his second administration. His appointment placed a major private financial executive in charge of a department responsible for trade, economic data, technology policy and numerous other federal responsibilities.

Lutnick's biography is relevant to the archive because it demonstrates the continuing role of Wall Street executives and major private-sector networks in Trump's political and administrative personnel system.

Pete Hegseth

Classification: Defense secretary; Army National Guard veteran; television commentator; political author.

Pete Hegseth served in the Army National Guard and became nationally known through conservative media before entering Trump's administration. He was a vocal Trump supporter during the first presidency and became increasingly prominent within Trump's political coalition.

Trump selected Hegseth to lead the Department of Defense during his second administration. His nomination generated extensive Senate scrutiny concerning his military record, management history, political statements and qualifications. He was nevertheless confirmed and became secretary of defense.

Hegseth's record demonstrates the continuing importance of media personalities and military veterans within Trump's political personnel network, particularly in national-security and defense policy.

Kash Patel

Classification: FBI director; former congressional investigator; former Trump administration national-security official.

Kash Patel first became prominent through his work as a congressional investigator and later joined the Trump administration. He served in several national-security roles, including positions at the National Security Council and Department of Defense. Patel became closely associated with Trump's efforts to challenge elements of the federal intelligence and law-enforcement establishment.

Patel subsequently became a prominent author and public defender of Trump. Trump nominated him to lead the FBI during the second administration. His confirmation became a major political controversy because of his previous criticism of the FBI and his stated views about the investigation of Trump and the 2016 election.

The Patel file is therefore useful for examining Trump's relationship with federal law enforcement, the political aftermath of the Russia investigation and the administration's effort to place trusted political allies in senior institutional positions.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

Classification: HHS secretary; environmental lawyer; former independent presidential candidate.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. initially entered the 2024 presidential campaign as a Democratic primary challenger before becoming an independent candidate. His campaign developed a coalition that extended beyond conventional Democratic politics and included supporters from different ideological backgrounds.

Kennedy eventually suspended his independent campaign and endorsed Donald Trump. Trump subsequently selected him to lead the Department of Health and Human Services. The appointment brought a longtime critic of portions of the federal health establishment into a cabinet-level position.

Kennedy's history includes extensive advocacy concerning vaccines, environmental regulation and public health. Those positions produced significant scientific and political controversy and became central issues during his confirmation process and subsequent tenure.

Dan Bongino

Classification: FBI deputy director; former Secret Service agent; political commentator; author.

Dan Bongino worked as a New York City police officer and later as a United States Secret Service agent before entering electoral politics and conservative media. He became a highly visible supporter of Trump and developed a large audience through radio, podcasts and online political commentary.

Bongino unsuccessfully ran for Congress multiple times before establishing himself as a prominent conservative media figure. His political relationship with Trump deepened during and after the first administration, and he became a regular defender of Trump during investigations and legal controversies.

Trump later selected Bongino for a senior FBI leadership position. His career provides an example of the movement from law enforcement to political media and then back into federal law enforcement through a Trump administration appointment.

Presidential Clemency and the Trump Network

Classification: Institutional archive of pardons and commutations associated with Donald Trump.

Presidential clemency is an important component of the personnel and political-network record because a pardon can remove or reduce federal criminal consequences without necessarily erasing the underlying historical record of the prosecution. The Department of Justice Office of the Pardon Attorney maintains the official database of Trump's clemency actions.

Trump's first presidency included clemency for several people with direct or indirect political relationships to him, including Roger Stone, Paul Manafort and Michael Flynn. His second presidency has included a substantially larger and continuing series of pardons and commutations. The DOJ database lists individual warrants, offenses, dates and forms of clemency and should be treated as the primary reference rather than relying solely on secondary summaries.

Among the most consequential examples is Ross Ulbricht, whom Trump granted a full and unconditional pardon on January 21, 2025. The same DOJ database records many other clemency actions, including pardons and commutations involving people convicted of financial, political, drug, election-related and other federal offenses. Each case should be researched individually rather than treated as part of one category.
